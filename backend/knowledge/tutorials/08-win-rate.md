This is the most important page in the course. If you leave with one correction, leave with this.

## The public number

The headline **Verified Win Rate** (the one on the landing page, on [Performance](/performance), and in the Signals header) is:

> the share of resolved calls that **reached at least TP1**.

A call that tagged TP1 and later stopped out still counts as a **win**. The rule is highest level reached: `tp4 > tp3 > tp2 > tp1 > sl`.

The table badge **WIN** is stricter: it means TP4 printed (`closed_win`). TP1 / TP2 / TP3 rows still count in this win rate. Do not audit the headline number by counting WIN badges.

Safe sentence: *"85.6% of our calls reached at least their first target."*

Forbidden sentence: *"85.6% profitable trades"* or *"85.6% of members made money."* We do not know your fill, your size, or whether you took the trade.

Today's Win Rate on Signals is narrower still: wins ÷ *closed* signals **created today**. A quiet morning with two closed calls can print 100% or 0%. Do not confuse it with the long-run number.

## There are two win rates. Do not stand them next to each other.

The engine can also compute a **closed** win rate: roughly the TP4 completion rate. That number is much lower because every call that stopped at TP1–TP3 is dropped from the denominator.

They share a name and almost nothing else. The public number — the one we put on the site, in captions, and in this course — is the **hit** definition (at least TP1). If someone quotes a ~57% "win rate" at you, they are on the other definition. Do not mix them under one heading.

## It is not the same in every market

On days Bitcoin drops more than 3%, the hit rate has historically sat around **71%**. On days it rips more than 3%, around **94%**. We will not claim the win rate is "consistent in every regime". We will claim it has stayed in the mid-80s year after year, and that 71% on a violent down day is still well above the ~34% breakeven implied by the ladder.

Past performance is a record, not a promise.
