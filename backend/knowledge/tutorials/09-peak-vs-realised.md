On [Home](/home) and in top-performer cards you will see numbers like **+313%**. Read the label.

## Two percentages, one coin

- **Peak (`gain_pct`)** — the highest price reached after the call, measured from entry, as a percent. The max run-up. The site already says this out loud: *"Peak is the max run-up during the trade … so it's not a realized gain."*
- **Realised (`realized_pct`)** — the gain to the **highest take-profit that actually printed** on that call.

They can diverge by an order of magnitude. A real example from the board: a coin showed **peak +313.79%** and **realised +13.3%**. Anyone who posted "+313%" as member profit was lying, even if the screenshot was real.

If a card uses a peak number, the word **peak** has to be on the card. We do not present peak as what you would have banked.

## Measured from the first call in the window

Some coins are called several times in a month. Peak on the 30-day board is measured from the **earliest** entry in that window to the subsequent high — not from whichever re-entry happened to be the cheapest. A Proof of Call card that shows the first and last call of seven must say there were seven. Showing 2 of 7 without the 7 is a mismatch.

## Leverage on social cards is a display convention

A caption that says "the 75x side caught 372%" is **spot percent × the `pnl_leverage` stored on the signal**. It is not a real position, not a member result, and it does not model liquidation. It is only honest if the words "at 75x" stay in the sentence.

## All-time records can be junk

The all-time "best call" board has contained artefacts (a +22,839% print we treat as unusable). Social cards use a **window** (30 days), not all-time, for "best call". If a number looks mythical, it probably wants a window, a peak label, and a grain of salt.
