[Performance](/performance) is how you audit us. It is free with a login on purpose.

## Three rooms

- **All-Time** — the long-run hit rate and totals. This is the number that should match the landing page.
- **Daily** — Overview, By Pattern, Correlation, By Sector, Today's Edge. Use this to see what is working *now*, not what worked in 2024.
- **Research (Edge Lab)** — Calibration (does high confidence actually win more?), Pattern × BTC, Expected Value, Calendar, Timing, Coins, WR × BTC.

## How to read a stat without lying to yourself

- **Win Rate** here is the same public definition: resolved calls that reached at least TP1. Colour: green is high, red is below 50%.
- **Hit Rate** is wins / total, shown as a fraction plus a percent — useful when the percent is jumpy because the denominator is small.
- **Resolved** means closed (win or loss). Open calls are not in the win rate. A 100% day with two closed calls is a coin flip wearing a suit.
- **EV** combines how often it hits with how large wins and losses have been. A high win rate with tiny wins and fat stops can still be a poor EV. A modest win rate with asymmetric payoffs can still be a good one.
- **Calibration** asks whether "high confidence" in the model actually came with more wins. If it does not, ignore the confidence and keep the record.
- **⚠ small sample** (n < 15 in most views) means do not build a personality around that breakdown.

## Pattern tags are correlations, not causes

A tag's historical win rate is "resolved calls that *carried* this tag". Tags overlap. In a long-only book during an up market, an "overbought" tag can historically win *more* — momentum continues — while "deep pullback" wins less. That is context. It is not a rule that says buy overbought.

If a breakdown only looks good in a "strong" regime that was itself labelled from our own daily win rate, it is circular. Prefer splits against **Bitcoin's** actual days, not against our own report card.
